# Paper outline (draft 1, 2026-11-05)

**Working title:** *Earning Every Feature: A Pre-Registered, Publicly Graded Forecasting Model for Weekly Fantasy Football*

**Audience:** someone who knows statistics or economics but not fantasy football (an admissions reader, a professor, an interviewer). Target length is 12–18 pages plus appendix. Every number comes from a file in `paper/`, regenerated by a script.

**The one-sentence claim:** a simple, interpretable model (opportunity × efficiency, empirical-Bayes shrinkage, betting-market information, median calibration) beats a strong trailing-average baseline on two untouched holdout seasons. Most "situational" ideas that sound smart do **not** survive honest testing, and the paper shows which and why.

## 1. Introduction
- **Why weekly fantasy projections are a forecasting problem:** loss function, skewed outcomes, small samples.
- **The problem with how projections are usually presented:** a single number, no stated method, no public accuracy record. *Phrase carefully: we can't see inside other sites' models.*
- **Contributions:**
  1. a fully walk-forward, pre-registered testing protocol;
  2. which components actually help, and by how much;
  3. calibrated uncertainty;
  4. a catalogue of plausible features that fail;
  5. a live, public head-to-head against a commercial projection source.
- **Roadmap** of the paper.

## 2. Background and related work *(every citation must be checked before use)*
- **Shrinkage / empirical Bayes:**
  - James–Stein;

- **Forecast evaluation:** Diebold & Mariano (1995); the block / cluster bootstrap.

- **Betting markets as forecasts:** the efficiency of NFL point spreads and totals (literature to be surveyed and verified).
- **Sports analytics practice:** multiple testing and the "garden of forking paths"; why pre-registration helps.

## 4. Model
Each step gets an equation, then one paragraph of economic intuition.
1. **Baseline:** EWMA of PPR points, half-life 8 games (`paper/results_shrinkage_ablation.md`).
2. **Opportunity × efficiency:**
   - decomposition by component (receiving, rushing, passing);
   - volume EWMA with shrinkage toward the player's long-run level;
   - efficiency shrunk toward the position rate: eff = (S_pts + k·μ) / (S_opp + k).
   - Fitted k: receiving 100, rushing 50, passing 200 pseudo-opportunities.
   - Show the **shrinkage ablation**: decomposition alone gives nothing; efficiency shrinkage is the main gain.
3. **Betting market:**
   - implied team points = (total ± spread)/2;
   - team volume regression;
   - efficiency multiplier 1 + α + β·(implied − mean).
   - The placebo separates information from structure (`results_betting_lines.md`, `results_eff_lines.md`).
4. **Median calibration:** LAD, multiplicative per position (v8 → v8m; why the intercept was dropped) (`results_median_cal*.md`).
5. **Uncertainty:** floor/ceiling via quantile regression with player volatility (v9 → v10) (`results_intervals*.md`).
6. **Explainability:** an additive waterfall per player; it sums exactly (`check_predict.md`).

## 5. Evaluation design
- **Walk-forward (forward-chained) folds;** no look-ahead, plus the checks that prove it (`check_live.md`).
- **The Big 60 pool:** the source's own top 10/20/20/10. Selection uses pre-game information only, so there's no selection on outcomes.
- **Metrics:** MAE (primary), RMSE, Spearman, coverage.
- **Paired bootstrap, clustered by week (or game):** why clustering matters.
- **Pre-registration:** the rule written in DECISIONS.md before each run; headline vs secondary comparisons.
- **Placebo tests:** the general method, and what they caught. Two cases: the regression confound in v4, and the prime-time fitting bug.

## 7. What didn't work (the section that makes the paper credible)
For each feature: the hypothesis, the pre-registered test, the result, the placebo, and why it likely failed.
- **Week-of-season ramp:** fixed late bias but worse overall.
- **Week 1 effect:** position and player level; includes the bug-fix re-run.
- **Prime time:** the placebo exposed a fitting bug, the fix, and then a null result.
- **Weather:** a null on top players; the dome pattern noted as an untested hypothesis.
- **Opponent strength:** τ² ≈ 0. Defensive "matchups" barely persist once shrunk; within-season stickiness ≈ +0.06.
- **Vacated opportunity:** tested twice; the placebo "helped" through recalibration until calibration was in the model.
- **Team context alone.**

**Discussion:** why markets and shrinkage already absorb most "situational" signal.

## 9. Limitations
- **Baseline-only holdout comparisons** (no free historical platform projections).
- **Comparator caveats:**
  - one live platform;
  - one season;
  - Sleeper's projections come via an undocumented endpoint (disclosed).
- **Questionable players** are projected at their normal role; late scratches.
- **Rookies with no history** are dropped (counted).
- **Possible remaining tuning on dev seasons,** with how pre-registration limits it.

## 10. Conclusion and future work
- **Use forecasts actually made before kickoff:** a weather re-test with stored pre-kickoff forecasts, and line movement.
- **Injury-status probabilities** for Questionable players.
- **More platforms** if terms allow.
